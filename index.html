Reading the meters

  • Network load — RMS tension across every element. 100 % is the body's own resting pre-tension, measured at start-up.
  • Peak rise — the largest local increase above rest anywhere in the network.
  • Fidelity — the fraction of the achievable signal that survives the tissue between the event and the ending.
  • Added latency — delay beyond what healthy tissue and normal conduction already cost.
  • Bandwidth — how much of the frequency range a class is built to resolve still reaches it.

Performance & quality

The model is fragment-bound rather than geometry-bound: a dozen translucent layers overlap at every pixel of the figure, while the simulation itself costs well under a millisecond a frame. Render ▸ Quality therefore works mostly on how many pixels are shaded and how expensive each one is. Auto measures the real frame time and holds 60 fps by moving the render scale first — the least visible lever — and only changing tier once that band is exhausted.

Tension colouring, rim lighting, force propagation, the signal streams and the living physiology are present at every tier — the tiers change how much is spent drawing them, never what is modelled. No tier is gated by licence. Geometry tessellation is the one setting chosen at load time from the detected hardware; the frame diagnostics report which was built, and a reload rebuilds it.

The quality choice and the diagnostics panel both persist across reloads, so a tier can be locked and measured without Auto moving underneath the test. The panel's log button lists every decision Auto made with the frame time that caused it, and copy puts a full report — GPU, viewport, tier, frame time, draw calls, triangles, CPU solve time and the whole decision log — on the clipboard. If the panel's GPU line reads SOFTWARE RASTERISER, the machine is not using its GPU and the frame times mean nothing about real hardware.

Research overlays

An overlay is a flat map from anatomical ID to a number — that is the whole contract, which is why an elastography export, a myotonometry session and a modelling result all arrive the same way. Load one with Research overlays ▸ load…, or click a dataset that ships with the build. Keys may be a canonical ID, an alias, or a region code such as THORAX or PLANTAR_L, which fans out to every structure in that region. Values may carry their dispersion and sample count, shown in the inspector as 62.5 ± 9.1 (n=12). Anything that cannot be resolved is reported rather than dropped — an overlay silently covering 30 of 50 structures is worse than one that says so. The overlay paints through a single per-structure uniform, so the living physiology and the tension mapping keep running underneath it.

How the platform is put together

  • A compact core engine. One biotensegrity solve per frame publishes displacement and tension into a small data texture that every tissue shader samples. That single solve drives the deformation and colour of every system, the firing of every receptor population and every number on the instrument strip.
  • Everything else attaches by ID. Each structure carries a permanent identifier — BONE_FEMUR_L, FASCIA_CERVICAL_DEEP, RECEPTOR_PACINIAN_PLANTAR_L_01 — shown in the inspector and copyable. Properties, live state, research datasets, parameter sets, notes, measurements and saved scenes all key off it, and none of them touch geometry or materials. A dataset that speaks these IDs paints onto the model without any code change, which is also what lets a scene saved today reload against a future revision of the model.
  • Two tiers. Explorer covers macro anatomy at whole-body and region scale, with the basic live instrument: network load, global tension, signal integrity and fidelity, breath excursion and fluid transport. Professional adds every scale, every layer, unlimited multi-select, mechanical intervention, tension mapping, signal streams, measurement, annotation, overlays, parameter sets, saved projects — and the advanced read-out: per-receptor bandwidth and latency, live viscoelastic parameters, left/right comparison and the afferent trace. The gate is enforced in the engine, not in the interface.

What changes as you descend

  • Layers peel, then re-form. Skin and superficial fascia fade out by the organ tier. Bone, muscle and deep fascia thin hard as you approach it — they are what you need to see past — reach their thinnest while you are travelling between the organ and tissue tiers, and then come partly back below that, because once you are inside the tissue it is the subject again and an ending has to be embedded in something to mean anything. Your layer switches are untouched throughout; this only scales how strongly a visible layer is drawn.
  • The view sections. From the organ tier inward the near plane advances toward the look-at point, and the tissue approaching it fades rather than being clipped, so the cut reads like a section rather than like missing geometry. Tissue well past the focal depth fades too, which is what stops the deep views becoming a wash of colour with nothing to read a silhouette against.
  • Receptor markers reach true size. Above the tissue tier the endings are drawn as legible markers — a 150 µm Meissner corpuscle is a fraction of a pixel at whole-body view. Below it they converge on their real dimensions, and the scale readout says so.
